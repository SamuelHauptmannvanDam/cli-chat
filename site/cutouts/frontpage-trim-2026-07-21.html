## 03 · go public

Public — One word opens the doors.

Every mode ships private: only your contacts reach your chat modes. Building a desk anyone may write to? Add one word: public.

["public"]

Walk-ins welcome

One word on any mode — chat public, draft chat public — and the desk accepts every new handle on its own: first message answered, sender saved, the held backlog let in too.

["auto chat public"]

The desk takes notes

Plain auto chat keeps its pen: the desk takes notes for the documentation in its directory as it sees fit — learnings, docs, an FAQ that grows — and today's files ground tomorrow's answers. A sender can never order a write; what's worth keeping is always the desk's own call.

["answers only"]

Look, don't touch

The outward-facing setting: answering is all the desk can do — a sender's words can't touch a file, run a tool, or flip a setting, and a message that tries is flagged for you, never obeyed. It answers from what it sees, so open a public desk in a directory you'd happily read aloud.

[per session]

Yours alone

Public lasts exactly one session — it ends when you close the terminal. "private" shuts the gate mid-session, and a dismissed handle stays out even now.

"auto chat answers only public"

An endless shift at the open desk

Watch a while: strangers walk in and are saved on arrival, grounded questions get answered and scroll away — but the tickets only you can decide park at the bottom with your name on them, and the desk keeps answering over their heads. One line from you at the prompt clears a ticket. The directory stays untouched: answers only.

an escalation never stops the line · "private" shuts the gate again · a dismissed handle stays out even now

[answers only]

Customer desk

Point it at your docs. Customers get grounded answers; the rest lands with you, whole exchange in hand. Invite-only by default — or go public and take walk-ins.

## 06 · meet the tools

You never call a tool. You say the thing.

One MCP server underneath, nothing to learn — every box here is a sentence that already works. Start with the essential one: a 6-character handle is your phone number.

[your handle]

Ask "what's my handle?"

Six characters, yours to share — say it on a call, drop it in a README. Swapping them once is the whole exchange.

[you write theirs]

The first message names it

write Sam at qL7fA: hey — the send itself saves him. From then on write Sam: hey is enough.

[they write yours]

Their first message knocks

A 🆕 notice, sealed until you say "add" — strangers never read themselves into your terminal. Running an open desk? "go public" lets them straight in for the session.

"write sam@gmail.com: hey"

Email works too

Any address is reachable. No account there yet? They get one invite mail — ever — and the message waits, sealed, until they join.

"who are my contacts?"

The book

Recents first, you on top. Rename anyone — yourself included — by saying it. Deleting forgets; it never blocks.

"write everyone from work"

Tags & group send

Private labels the agent applies from context. A fan-out always shows you the roster before anything sends.

"verify Niels"

Safety numbers

60 digits, compared outside the wire. If their key ever changes you see 🚩 before anything sends.

"give me a new code"

Rotate the handle

Spammed? New code, old one dies, every saved contact keeps working. Or "kill my handle": connect requests only.

"connect with the Tobias Niels knows"

Friends of friends

See your second circle by name; reach them through a connect request they accept — "any requests?" shows who's asking for you. Nothing delivers before consent.

"log in"

One account, every machine

Your email is the account. Contacts, tags, history and memory land on any device, encrypted — "log out" wipes the box.

"remember: staging is db-2"

The memory bank

Say it once, never again. Notes carry an audience — private, a tag, anyone — and ground every auto answer.

"what do you share about me?"

Default-closed

Nothing personal goes out without a rule you approved. Inspect the ruleset any time; change it in a sentence.

"auto chat answers only"

Look, don't touch

The outward desk posture: it answers from the directory but can never write to it. "write mode" upgrades it back — only from your keyboard, never from a message.

"go public"

Open the doors

New handles walk straight in for the session; "private" shuts the gate again. Every mode has it — the doors, above.

"stop"

Closing time

Ends any running mode. Messages simply queue, sealed, until next time — a desk's session is its opening hours, and nothing is ever lost.
